Current Agricultural Context & Challenges

Bangladesh's legal framework is anchored in the Pesticide Act of 2018, which mandates registration, licensing, labeling, and safe handling, overseen by the Pesticide Technical Advisory Committee (PTAC). However, as of mid-2026, no final rules have been published from recommendations made by the FAO in 2024 — indicating weak implementation and enforcement of existing legislation.

⚠ Critical Challenge: A survey found that 68% of farmers had used pesticides officially banned in Bangladesh, while 93% were unaware of these bans or the associated health risks.

Researchers at Bangladesh Agricultural University identified 25 highly hazardous pesticides registered for use in Bangladesh according to the FAO. Ten of these — including paraquat, glyphosate, chlorpyrifos, and carbendazim — are widely used and linked to cancer, kidney failure, heart disease, and reproductive disorders.

📈 Alarming Trend: Insecticide application has surged by more than 80% in the past five years, yet pests still damage crops and harvests remain uncertain. Meanwhile, post-harvest losses cost an estimated $2.4 billion annually, driven largely by the absence of cold-chain infrastructure.

Regulatory Reform & Registration Pathways

A key barrier is the absence of a dedicated regulatory framework for biopesticides, creating uncertainty in registration and approval. Complex, time-consuming approval processes and stringent data requirements deter manufacturers from entering the market.

Short-term Actions (1–3 Years)

  • Finalize updated Pesticide Rules under the 2018 Act in collaboration with FAO, incorporating international best practices.
  • Establish a transparent registration process with rigorous risk assessments to keep highly hazardous pesticides out of the market.
  • Align national frameworks with ASEAN Guidelines on Biological Control Agents to reduce trade barriers.

Medium-term Structural Reforms (5–10 Years)

  • Establish a stand-alone definition of biopesticides in national law, or a dedicated chapter, so they are not subsumed under chemical-pesticide rules.
  • Introduce flexible registration categories: provisional, emergency-use, and minor-use registration.
  • Facilitate parallel and equivalent registration for products approved by recognized authorities (US, EU, UK, Japan, Australia).
  • Set clear decision timeframes (≤120 days) and robust post-registration stewardship, including mandatory sales reporting and revocation mechanisms.

Supply Chain & Manufacturing Incentives

Bangladesh's agro-input marketing suffers from a fragmented supply chain, limited transportation, high costs, and inefficient distribution. Biopesticides' specific storage needs and shorter shelf lives complicate distribution — especially in remote areas — making cold-chain investment essential.

Short-term (1–3 Years)

  • • Train local manufacturers in production techniques and business-model development.
  • • Partner with distributors and cooperatives to improve last-mile accessibility.
  • • Engage aggregators and market actors so the whole chain understands biopesticide value.

Medium-term (5–10 Years)

  • • Standardize production protocols for consistent quality.
  • • Establish a national biobank of microbial stocks (Trichoderma, Bt).
  • • Build climate-controlled storage & distribution nationwide.
  • • Grow private-sector capacity via public-private partnerships.

Farmer Adoption & Extension Services

Farmers often question biopesticide efficacy due to slower action and environment-sensitive performance. Higher costs and lack of subsidies make them less attractive. Yet engagement with agricultural extension services is associated with a 4.2% increase in technology adoption and a 2.4% decrease in production risk among rice farmers.

Digital Extension (e-Extension)

CABI and the Department of Agricultural Extension (DAE) are piloting Bangladesh's first Strategic Action Plan for e-Extension — a three-tiered framework (National Desks, Regional Hubs, Satellite Hubs) delivering real-time advice via SMS, IVR, mobile apps, and platforms like PlantwisePlus, BAMIS, and Krishoker Janala.

💰 Economic Incentives: Implement subsidies, tax breaks, and low-interest credit to offset higher costs and demonstrate long-term benefits like reduced pest resistance.

Implementation Roadmap

Interventions must be sequenced: regulatory clarity precedes supply-chain development, which enables effective farmer adoption through strengthened extension services.

Conclusion & Strategic Recommendations

Pesticide use growth in Bangladesh slowed dramatically — from a 297% increase (2001–2010) to only about 17% (2010–2023) — with rising biopesticide use cited as a contributing factor. This framework provides a comprehensive roadmap balancing immediate regulatory reforms with long-term structural transformation for food security, environmental sustainability, and farmer prosperity.

Key Recommendations

  • Prioritize Regulatory Clarity: finalize biopesticide-specific rules within 2 years.
  • Invest in Human Capital: train 10,000+ extension workers within 5 years.
  • Build Supply-Chain Resilience: partner with 500+ local distributors.
  • Enable Financial Access: targeted subsidies and low-interest credit.
  • Foster Innovation: establish biopesticide innovation hubs for tropical formulations.
